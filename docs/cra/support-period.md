# Support period (Article 13(8), Annex VII point 4)

## Proposal, for the manufacturer to decide

**Five years from the date each version is placed on the market.** Article 13(8)
sets five years as the minimum unless the product is expected to be in use for
less. An editor for long-term writing projects is likely to stay in use longer
than that, so there is no ground for a shorter period.

| Version | Placed on the market | End of support |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1.0 | October 2026| October 2031 |

## What was considered

- **Expected use:** a document editor is used for years; manuscripts outlive releases.
- **User expectations:** comparable desktop editors receive updates for years.
- **Components:** Go supports its two latest releases, so each new Go version must be adopted; Gio is pinned and patched in `third_party`, and its fixes must be followed by hand.
- **Operating environment:** current Linux, FreeBSD, macOS and browsers.
- **Capacity:** one person. A later successor version may take over support if users of older versions can update to it free of charge (Article 13(10)).

## Duties during the period

- Handle vulnerabilities as in [vulnerability-handling.md](vulnerability-handling.md).
- Keep each security update available for 10 years after release, or the rest of the period if longer (Article 13(9)).
- State the end date, at least month and year, at the time of purchase (Article 13(19)).
- If technically feasible, show users that support has ended (Article 13(19)); this comes with the update check (open item B1).
- If the business stops, tell the market surveillance authority and users beforehand (Article 13(23)).
